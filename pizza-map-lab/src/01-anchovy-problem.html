The Anchovy Problem

A classifier looked down at a pizza and made a topping map. Is the map any good? That depends on who is asking. The person eating wants to know if a spot marked "anchovy" really has one. The person who built the classifier wants to know how many of the real anchovies it found. Those are two different questions with two different answers.

It's a sheet pizza cut into 64 squares, like a raster cut into image chips. It was baked so the map's mistakes match the paper's own example exactly: classes A, B, C and D in Table 5 are cheese, mushroom, pepperoni and anchovy here.

The error matrix

Every spot on the pizza lands in one cell: its row is what the map said, its column is what is really there. Click a cell to light up those spots on both pizzas.

Each cell shows pizza pixels, with the share of the whole pizza underneath. With the paper's map those shares are exactly Table 5 of Stehman & Foody (2019). We checked every pixel, so these are the true values for this pizza, not estimates.

Two people, two questions

The eater reads across a row. Take everything the map calls anchovy and ask what share is really anchovy. False alarms drag this down.

user's accuracy = diagonal ÷ row total
The paper's Eq. 3class 4 is anchovy (D)
U_i = p_{ii}/p_{i+}
U_{\id{q-u-i}{4}} = p_{\id{q-u-ii}{44}}/p_{\id{q-u-ir}{4}+} = \id{q-u-a}{461}/\id{q-u-b}{720} = \id{q-u-v}{64.0}\%

Each p is a share of the whole pizza. Both shares are pixel counts divided by 14,400, so the ratio of the counts in the matrix is the same number.

The mapmaker reads down a column. Take every real anchovy and ask what share the map labelled anchovy. Misses drag this down.

producer's accuracy = diagonal ÷ column total
The paper's Eq. 4class 4 is anchovy (D)
P_j = p_{jj}/p_{+j}
P_{\id{q-p-j}{4}} = p_{\id{q-p-jj}{44}}/p_{+\id{q-p-jc}{4}} = \id{q-p-a}{461}/\id{q-p-b}{1066} = \id{q-p-v}{43.2}\%

A plus sign in a subscript means "add up that way": p_{i+} is a row total and p_{+j} is a column total.

Every mistake counts twice

A spot that is really mushroom but mapped as anchovy is a commission error for anchovy and an omission error for mushroom. Point at a mistake on either pizza to see both readings.

The paper's map is two different maps

For anchovy it is trustworthy where it speaks (64% user's) but finds fewer than half the real anchovies (43% producer's). Most of the missing ones are mapped as mushroom. An eater and a mapmaker would describe this map very differently.

Rare toppings are fragile

Anchovy is 7.4% of the pizza. Press Sees anchovies everywhere: the map finds every real anchovy, and its false alarms are only 8% of the rest of the pizza. That sounds small, but 8% of everything else is about as many spots as all the real anchovies, so the eater's question turns into a coin flip.

Overall accuracy can't see any of this

Press Never says anchovy. The map finds zero anchovies and overall accuracy barely moves, because anchovies are a sliver of the pizza. That is why the paper asks for user's and producer's accuracy, or the full matrix, every time.

The paper's Eq. 2c = 4 toppings
O = \sum_{i=1}^{c} p_{ii}
O = p_{11}+p_{22}+p_{33}+p_{44}
\phantom{O} = \frac{\id{q-o-1}{7258}+\id{q-o-2}{2592}+\id{q-o-3}{1152}+\id{q-o-4}{461}}{14400} = \id{q-o-v}{79.6}\%

One sum down the diagonal. Anchovy's p_{44} is the smallest term, so losing it barely moves O.

Based on Stehman, S.V. & Foody, G.M. (2019). Key issues in rigorous accuracy assessment of land cover products. Remote Sensing of Environment 231, 111199, Section 4.1, Eqs. 2–4 and Table 5. doi:10.1016/j.rse.2019.05.018
