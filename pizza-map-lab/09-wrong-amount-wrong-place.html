Wrong Amount, Wrong Place

The map gets 20.4% of the pizza wrong (Table 5: overall accuracy 79.6%). That one number hides two different sins. The map puts down too much cheese and too little anchovy: the wrong amount. And even where the amounts are right, it drops toppings on the wrong spots. Pontius and Millones split the 20.4 into quantity and allocation (Eqs. 6 to 11), and Pontius and Santacruz split allocation again into exchange (two spots that traded labels) and shift (everything else). This page pulls the map's mistakes apart those three ways, and lets you add mistakes of exactly one kind.

The map's share minus the real share is Eq. 5, map bias (06 was all about it). Its absolute value is a topping's quantity disagreement. Here pk+ is the row total and p+k the column total, both in points.

Eq. 7 · overall quantityhalved, because every point counts twice
Q=∑k=1cqk/2
Q=(4.6+1.4+0.8+2.4)/2=4.6

Cheese, mushroom, pepperoni, anchovy. Too much of one topping is too little of another, so the sum of the four is twice the overall quantity disagreement.

Eqs. 8 and 9 · allocationanchovy (class 4)
ak=2min⁡[p+k−pkk,pk+−pkk],A=∑k=1cak/2
a4=2min⁡[4.2,1.8]=3.6,A=15.8

The smaller of a topping's omission (rest of its column) and commission (rest of its row), twice: the part of its mistakes that the amounts can't explain. ak is also dk−qk, so each topping's allocation is its exchange plus its shift.

Eqs. 10 and 11 · total disagreementPontius & Millones (2011)
D=1−∑k=1cpkk=Q+A
D=100−79.6=20.4=4.6+15.8

One minus overall accuracy, in points. The paper's Table 5 gives 79.6% overall, so the map is wrong on 20.4% of the pizza.

P&S Eq. 3 · exchange between a pairmushroom–cheese
εij=2min⁡(Cij,Cji)for i>j
ε2,1=2min⁡(4.0,7.2)=8.0

Pontius & Santacruz write the cells as Cij, row i (the map) and column j (the reference), the same way round as the paper's Table 3. Each mirror pair is counted once, so the sum over pairs is the pizza's exchange.

P&S Eqs. 1, 4 and 5 · one topping's exchange and shiftanchovy (class 4)
dj=∑i(Cij+Cji)−2Cjj,ej=2[∑imin⁡(Cij,Cji)−Cjj],sj=dj−qj−ej
d4=5.0+7.4−2×3.2=6.0
e4=2[4.0−3.2]=1.6,s4=6.0−2.4−1.6=2.0

A topping's difference is its row plus its column, minus the diagonal twice. Its exchange adds up its mirror-pair minimums, with the diagonal taken back out. Shift is whatever is left. For error assessment the time terms in their Eqs. 1 to 4 drop out (p. 7548).

Step 3 · Merge two toppings

Pontius and Santacruz's biggest exchange was between coniferous and deciduous forest: 6.49% of the town of Ipswich. Call both "forest" and that error vanishes (Sec. 4, p. 7550). What the papers say

Two sins, not one

Quantity disagreement is the part of the map's error that comes from getting the amounts wrong; allocation disagreement is the rest, the mistakes that remain once the amounts are matched as well as they can be (Pontius & Millones 2011; Eqs. 6 to 11, p. 11). Map bias (Eq. 5) can sit near zero while both error rates are high, because omission and commission cancel (Sec. 4.1). That is exactly a map with quantity 0 and allocation high.

D = quantity + exchange + shift

Exchange and shift

Pontius and Santacruz split allocation again. Exchange is allocation that pairwise confusion explains: for every spot mapped A that is really B, a spot mapped B that is really A. Shift is allocation that no pairing explains, and needs at least three classes (their Sec. 2.1). Stehman and Foody mention both as a way to supplement, not replace, the matrix and the user's and producer's accuracies (p. 11).

What each one hints at

A spectral signature that is too narrow or too broad gives quantity error; two classes with similar signatures give exchange (their Sec. 4, p. 7550). They suggest fixing quantity first and then looking at the components again, because fixing amounts moves the other two as well. The anchovy slider on 06, read backwards, is this page's quantity.

The caveats

Exchange is a count from the matrix, not a list of spot pairs that traded places: no two spots on this pizza actually swapped. Stehman and Foody also warn that omission and commission are not interchangeable, so measures that pair pij with pji should sit next to the class-specific accuracies, not replace them (pp. 10–11). And these are census numbers from every spot; from a sample they would be estimated from the estimated matrix (Pontius & Santacruz did this with a stratified sample, p. 7549) and would carry standard errors of their own.
